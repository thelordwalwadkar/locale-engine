About the centrifugal pump

A centrifugal pump consists of a pump casing containing a rotating impeller. In this principle the liquid to be pumped is, as it were, flung away by the rotating impeller. As the liquid is thrown outwards, space is created on the inside (also called negative pressure). New liquid can flow into this space, which keeps the pump working continuously.

Centrifugal pumps can be supplied as a single pump (with or without a motor) or as a complete pump set.

Besides the pump and motor themselves, a pump set consists of a baseplate and any other ancillary equipment.

How does a centrifugal pump work?

The centrifugal pump, which consists of a pump casing, an impeller and an electric motor, among other parts, works by means of centrifugal force, which moves the liquid. This force is generated by the impeller, or rotor, of the pump. It sits in the pump casing, which has a suction flange and a discharge flange.

When the pump is started, the liquid in the pump begins to rotate with the impeller and is subject to centrifugal force. It therefore flows into the pump casing at the outer edge of the impeller and then, through the discharge opening, into the discharge line. These pumps cannot run dry (that is, without liquid); the advantages of the centrifugal pump are described at the bottom of the page.

How does this problem arise?

Cavitation in centrifugal pumps

Centrifugal pumps are at risk of cavitation, which is the formation and collapse of vapour bubbles in liquid water. Cavitation occurs when the pressure in the pump falls below the vapour pressure of the liquid. The imploding vapour bubbles in the water cause (a lot of) noise and vibration, and then mechanical shocks. Cavitation damages various components such as the impeller and the pump casing, which naturally does not benefit the life of the pump.

Cavitation can be prevented if the inlet pressure (NPSHa) of the pump remains higher than the vapour pressure (NPSHr) of the pump. The NPSHa value must be at least 1 mWC higher than the NPSHr value. Again, it is very important that the pump selected is suitable for your application. Would you like advice on this? Then please contact us.

SPARE PARTS AND REPAIR OF CENTRIFUGAL PUMPS

A centrifugal pump has few moving parts, so maintenance costs for this type of pump are generally lower. The parts that wear most are the impeller and the seals (also called the mechanical seal).

If you want maintenance carried out on your (centrifugal) pump, it is important that this is done properly. With its range of pump experts, IPG has enough knowledge in-house to support you with the overhaul of your pumps.
